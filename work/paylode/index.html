Case Study 03 — Payments · Banking · Dashboards

Paylode Services

End-to-end design leadership for a payments company — consumer bills, payment gateways, and the operations dashboards behind them.

PL
Role
Lead UI/UX Designer
Timeline
Jul 2022 — Oct 2023
Company

01

Overview

Paylode operates in Nigeria's online payments space — bills payment, payment gateway services and banking-adjacent financial products. As Lead UI/UX Designer I led and contributed to the design and development of multiple user-centred, end-to-end interfaces across that portfolio.

Payments design is a two-sided problem: the consumer paying a bill in thirty seconds, and the operations staff reconciling thousands of those payments behind the scenes. Both sides deserved interfaces designed with the same rigour.

02

The Challenge

  • Money moments tolerate zero confusion. In bills payment and gateways, every ambiguous state — pending, failed, reversed — becomes a support ticket or a lost customer.
  • The back office was invisible. Operations staff needed administrative dashboards with the features to work efficiently, but nobody had gathered their requirements.
  • A public-sector side quest. A Lagos State scholarship portal needed to register and manage available scholarships and applicants — accessible to a broad, non-technical audience.
03

Process

01

Requirements with cross-functional teams

I collaborated with cross-functional teams and stakeholders to gather requirements and translate them into effective design solutions — sitting with product, engineering and business owners to turn "we need a gateway dashboard" into defined screens, states and edge cases.

02

Designing for the money moment

For the consumer-facing payment flows, I designed every state explicitly — success, pending, failure, reversal — so users always knew where their money stood. The interface copy did the reassurance work; the visuals did the clarity work.

03

The back office, designed properly

I worked closely with staff to gather the requirements that made certain the administrative dashboards included features that enabled them to work efficiently — built from their actual daily workflows rather than assumptions about them. Internal tools are products too; treating them that way cut the friction in operations work.

04

Seamless handoff to developers

I worked closely with developers to ensure seamless implementation of designs — staying available through the build, resolving ambiguity early, and reviewing implemented screens against the design intent rather than throwing specs over a wall.

05

Lagos State scholarship portal

Alongside the core product work, I helped design a Lagos State scholarship portal to register and manage available scholarships and applicants — a public-facing flow designed for first-time internet users as much as for administrators.

04

Outcome

A coherent payments portfolio

Multiple end-to-end interfaces for bills payment, gateways and banking products — designed as one system, not disconnected screens.

Operations that work

Administrative dashboards shaped by the staff who use them daily, with the features their workflows actually need.

Public-sector reach

A Lagos State scholarship portal designed for a broad audience — from applicants to administrators.

05

Learnings

  • Design every state, or the user designs it for you. In payments, the unhappy paths are the product.
  • Internal tools deserve research. An afternoon watching operations staff work beat weeks of guessing at dashboard requirements.
  • Handoff is a relationship, not a deliverable. Staying close to developers through the build is what "seamless implementation" actually requires.
