Case Study 06 — Search · Open Access · Research

Cloud-based OPAC

My undergraduate thesis product — a cloud library catalogue that puts open-access research alongside the physical shelves.

OC
Role
Designer & Project Lead
Timeline
2025 — Undergraduate Thesis
Institution
Tai Solarin Federal University of Education, Ijagun
Platform
Web — cloud prototype
Domain
Library & Information Science
01

Overview

My final-year project, awarded First Class Honours: designing and directing the development of a prototype cloud-based Online Public Access Catalogue (OPAC) for academic libraries in Nigeria.

Traditional library catalogues tell you what's on the physical shelves — and stop there. Meanwhile, a world of open-access research sits a click away, invisible to catalogue searches. This project merged the two: one search box that returns physical library holdings and relevant open-access resources together.

02

The Challenge

  • Two information worlds, one interface. Physical library metadata and open-access digital resources are structured, stored and licensed differently — the catalogue had to unify them without confusing either.
  • Real constraints. Academic libraries in Nigeria often operate with limited infrastructure — the "cloud-based" architecture wasn't a buzzword, it was a necessity for accessibility and cost.
  • Academic rigour plus product craft. As a thesis, it had to meet research standards; as a product, it had to actually serve students and librarians.
03

Process

01

Framing the information problem

The project began with the core information-science question: how should search results present resources from two different universes — shelf metadata and open-access records — so a student can tell, at a glance, what's physically available versus what's instantly accessible online?

02

Designing the unified search

I designed the prototype around a single search experience that integrated open-access resources into results alongside physical library meta information — with clear source labelling, availability states, and paths to access for each result type.

03

Directing the prototype build

I designed and directed the development of the working prototype — making the architecture and interaction decisions, and steering the build toward demonstrating the thesis argument in software rather than slides.

A thesis prototype has one job: make the argument real enough to evaluate. Every feature either served that or got cut.
04

Outcome

A working prototype

A functional cloud-based OPAC demonstrating unified search across physical holdings and open-access resources.

First Class Honours

The thesis contributed to graduating as Best Graduating Student with a CGPA of 4.52 — First Class Honours.

Where it all connects

This project is the origin story of my product practice — information architecture, user needs and technical constraints resolved into one shippable thing.

05

Learnings

  • Information architecture is product design. How you structure and label information determines what users can find, trust and use.
  • Constraints clarify. Limited infrastructure and a hard deadline forced decisions that a bigger budget would have let me defer — badly.
  • Lead the build, don't just spec it. Directing development taught me how design decisions land in code — a perspective I've used ever since.
